# Idle Games: A Synopsis

## Overview

Idle games (also known as incremental, clicker, or tap games) are a genre of video games focused on minimal player interaction. Players perform simple actions (like clicking a button) to generate in-game currency, which can be spent on upgrades that automate progress, leading to exponential growth in resources.

## Core Mechanics

- **Idling:** Progress continues with little or no player input, often through automated systems.
- **Exponential Growth:** Currency and rewards escalate rapidly, often requiring scientific notation or shorthand for large numbers.
- **Prestige Systems:** Players can reset progress for permanent bonuses, creating new gameplay loops and strategic decisions.
- **Goals & Achievements:** Frequent small rewards and achievements keep players engaged and provide direction.
- **Open/Closed Loops:** Some games are endless (e.g., Cookie Clicker), while others have defined endings (e.g., Candy Box!).
- **Monetization:** Many idle games use microtransactions or ads for minor in-game rewards.

## History

- **Early Examples:** Progress Quest (2002) is considered the first idle game, followed by titles like Cow Clicker (2010) and Candy Box! (2013).
- **Popularization:** Cookie Clicker (2013) and AdVenture Capitalist (2015) brought the genre mainstream, introducing features like offline progression and layered prestige.
- **Evolution:** Idle mechanics have influenced other genres, introducing auto-play and progression loops in RPGs, strategy, and simulation games.

## Reception

Idle games are praised for their low-pressure, distraction-friendly gameplay and constant sense of progress. Critics sometimes describe them as "glorified spreadsheets" but acknowledge their broad appeal and influence on game design.
